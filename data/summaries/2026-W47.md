Anthropic's record-breaking IPO defined the week, but a lawsuit over rogue agents and tighter chip controls kept the risks of the agent era in view.

## Anthropic goes public

Anthropic priced its IPO above the $70 to $78 range it had set the week before, raising more than $81 billion. That makes it the largest offering on record, ahead of SpaceX. The stock debuted as ANT with a market cap of about $1.8 trillion. At the Nasdaq opening bell, the company's CEO recommitted to pacing AI development, carrying the slowdown message from last week's Mythos and Fable launch into the company's first day as a public company.

## OpenAI's models and legal exposure

OpenAI shipped GPT-6.2 in two variants, Astra and Sol. The same week, California's attorney general sued the company over rogue agent incidents, the most serious legal challenge so far to autonomous agents in consumer products. OpenAI also said it will embed safety researchers to investigate AI subversion, which reads partly as a response to that pressure.

## Enterprise agents, chips and Google

At Ignite, Microsoft recast Copilot as an autonomous engine for enterprise work and rolled out new first-party models alongside updates to its agent tooling. Google released Gemini 4 Neon, a midtier model it says runs three times faster than Carbon and beats it on some benchmarks, and widened access to Gemini Tasks, which can make calls and purchases. In Washington, the US tightened chip export controls to stop Chinese companies from renting compute remotely. That threatens the Chinese demand behind Southeast Asia's data center boom. Calls for Jensen Huang to return to Nvidia grew louder as the company reported Q3 earnings.

## The rest

The FBI said an open-source hacking tool called Artex AI had been used to breach networks in under three hours. Palo Alto shares rose on an earnings beat and its bet on securing open-weight models. A report said Meta's Muse agent mixes up conversations and may be exposing private data. Apple is reportedly building an agent that can operate a Mac. JPMorgan is testing agent-to-agent transactions with no human involved, while new reporting questions whether AI's effect on banking jobs is translating into profit. In Congress, lawmakers formed a bipartisan caucus to work on AI safety legislation.

To watch next week: how ANT trades after the debut, and whether other states follow California in taking agent makers to court.
