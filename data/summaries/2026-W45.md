This was a week where AI's financing and market risk overshadowed product news, with a sharp tech sell-off landing alongside a run of deal-making.

The most consequential story was financial. On Thursday the Magnificent 7 lost roughly $1 trillion in market value in a single session, and earlier in the week AI data center stocks and Musk's SpaceX fell after a Texas governor result raised concerns about a slowdown in the buildout. The pressure on infrastructure spending was visible elsewhere: Oracle filed to sell $20 billion in new stock and bonds to fund its AI expansion, a reminder of how much of the buildout depends on capital markets staying receptive. On the supply side, Lumentum's CEO described an "enormous" opportunity in a large AI connectivity deal with Marvell, underscoring that optical and networking components remain a central bottleneck and growth area.

On the product side, OpenAI released a new cybersecurity model, though coverage questioned whether it goes far enough to address the risks such capabilities raise. The company also moved to simplify ChatGPT by merging its Chat and Work tabs into a single experience.

Developer tooling continued to attract acquirers. Meta bought AI coding startup Conductor, while OpenAI agreed to acquire developer tooling startup Pierre Computer, both signs that the major labs are consolidating talent and products around coding workflows.

To watch next week: whether the market sell-off deepens or stabilizes, and how it affects financing plans like Oracle's.
